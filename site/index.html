SEC. 01 Existing conditions

Why the lead problem never actually gets solved

Nothing here is a marketing problem in the abstract. It's four specific leaks, and every contractor we talk to has at least two of them.

Shared lead marketplaces

Angi, Thumbtack and Networx sell the same homeowner to four contractors at once. You're not selling your workmanship, you're bidding against three guys who will go lower. The lead cost is the small part; the margin you give up to win it is the real bill.

Referrals don't forecast

Word of mouth builds a great business and a terrible calendar. It shows up when it shows up. January doesn't care how good your July was, and neither does payroll.

Boosting posts isn't advertising

A boosted job-site photo reaches people who already follow you. It does not reach the homeowner two zip codes over whose roof started leaking last week. Different objective, different audience, different campaign structure.

Leads go cold in the truck

Contact rates fall off a cliff after the first five minutes, and most contractors return calls at 7pm from the cab. Half of what looks like a bad lead source is really a follow-up problem — and it's the cheapest one on this list to fix.

SEC. 02 Scope of work

One channel, run properly

We only run Meta — Facebook and Instagram. No SEO retainers, no six-service bundle where five of them get ten minutes a week. Paid social is where homeowners who weren't searching yet can still be sold, and it is the whole job here.

  • 01

    Offer engineering

    "Free estimate" is not an offer — every competitor on the block gives one. We build something a homeowner has to think about: a financing angle, a seasonal tune-up at a real price, a no-breakdown guarantee, an inspection with a written report. The offer moves cost per estimate further than targeting ever will.

  • 02

    Job-site creative

    We script and edit ads from footage of your crews, your trucks and your finished work — shot on a phone, directed by us. Homeowners are hiring people they will let into their house, and stock photos don't get that job. New creative every month, because ad fatigue is what kills accounts in month three.

  • 03

    Radius and ticket targeting

    Campaigns built around your actual service radius, the zip codes with the housing stock and home values that match your ticket, and exclusions for the areas your crews hate driving to. Your leads, exclusively yours — never resold.

  • 04

    Speed-to-lead follow-up

    Automatic text and email the second a form comes in, then a call script for whoever picks up your phone, and reminders until the estimate is on the calendar. We wire it into your CRM or set one up.

  • 05

    Reporting on the number that pays you

    Conversion tracking installed properly, every lead tagged back to the ad that produced it, and a Monday report that reads: spend, leads, booked estimates, cost per booked estimate, and jobs closed once you tell us what closed. Clicks and impressions are diagnostics, not results, and we don't dress them up as results.

SEC. 03 Sequence of work

How the first 90 days run

These phases are in order because each one needs the one before it. Nobody scales a campaign in week one, and anyone who tells you otherwise is spending your money to find out.

01

Offer & market audit

Week 1

We go through your ticket sizes, close rate, service radius, capacity and what your competitors are already running in your market. Output is the offer, the audience, and the number we're driving to.

02

Creative production

Week 1–2

One shot list, one filming day on your job site — phone footage is fine. We cut it into a first batch of ads, write the copy, build the landing page and install tracking end to end.

03

Launch & learn

Week 2–4

Campaigns go live and we test angles against each other on real spend. Follow-up automations turn on the same day. Expect the first booked estimates inside two weeks and volatile numbers until the account has enough data.

04

Scale what closed

Month 2 onward

Now we optimize against closed jobs instead of leads, kill the angles that book but never close, expand the radius or the budget where the margin is, and refresh creative monthly so the account doesn't fatigue.

SEC. 08 Questions

Straight answers

How much do I need to spend on ads?

$[MIN SPEND] a month is the floor, and that's about learning speed, not greed — below it Meta doesn't get enough conversions to optimize and you spend three months finding out what two weeks should have told you. High-ticket trades like roofing and remodeling usually settle higher once the math works.

Are the leads exclusive to me?

Yes. They come from your own ad account and go straight into your CRM. Nothing is resold, and nobody else in your trade and market is running our campaigns against you.

How fast will I see booked estimates?

First leads usually land within days of launch, first booked estimates inside two weeks. The number stabilizes around week four once the account has data, and month three is where it typically pays best. If you need work on the calendar this week, ads are the wrong tool — call your past customers.

Do I need a website first?

No. We build a dedicated landing page for the campaign, which converts better than a homepage anyway. If you have a site, we'll use it where it helps and route ad traffic to the page built for it.

Who makes the ads?

We do — scripts, copy and editing. What we need from you is raw footage: crews working, before and afters, you talking to the camera for sixty seconds. Phone video is fine and usually outperforms polished production. We'll send a shot list so nobody guesses.

Am I locked into a contract?

A 90-day initial term, because ending sooner means paying for the learning period and leaving before the payoff. After that it's month to month with 30 days' notice.

What if I already tried Facebook ads and it flopped?

Common, and usually one of four things: boosted posts instead of campaigns, a generic offer, no tracking so nothing could be optimized, or leads nobody called back. We'll pull up your old account on the call and tell you which one it was.

What do you need from me each month?

Roughly an hour: a short check-in, whatever footage you gathered, and telling us which estimates closed. That last part is what lets us optimize toward revenue instead of leads.
